\chapter{Evaluation}\label{ch:evaluation}

This chapter presents the empirical evidence gathered during the development of the visual fact extraction subsystem and the runtime infrastructure that supports it.
It is divided into two clearly delineated parts.
Part~A (Section~\ref{sec:eval-tech}) reports technical validation results that are backed by benchmark data, dataset statistics, and training artifacts currently present in the repository.
Part~B (Section~\ref{sec:eval-planned}) describes the planned human-subject evaluation that constitutes the original research vision of this thesis but has not yet been conducted; it is included here to document the evaluation design against which future work can be measured.

\section{Current Technical Results}\label{sec:eval-tech}

The technical evaluation addresses a single core question: can a LoRA-fine-tuned vision-language model (VLM) reliably extract structured visual facts from F/A-18C cockpit screenshots, and does the resulting adapter substantially outperform the untuned base model?
The question is assessed through a series of benchmark comparisons on data that the model has never seen during training (holdout sets with verified zero overlap).
The metrics reported below are computed by the automated benchmark harness described in Section~\ref{sec:meth-benchmark} and are drawn directly from the benchmark artifacts stored in the repository.

The visual fact extraction task evolved across two generations of fact ontology.
The first generation (v1) defined 8 visual facts and was used to establish the initial training and evaluation pipeline.
The second generation (v2) expanded the ontology to 13 visual facts, aligning it with the runtime visual contract defined in \texttt{packs/fa18c\_startup/vision\_facts.yaml}.
The 13 facts cover panel-level page visibility (e.g., \texttt{tac\_page\_visible}, \texttt{supt\_page\_visible}, \texttt{fcs\_page\_visible}), fine-grained state indicators within panels (\texttt{fcsmc\_intermediate\_result\_visible}, \texttt{fcsmc\_in\_test\_visible}, \texttt{fcsmc\_final\_go\_result\_visible}), and small-text recognition targets (\texttt{ins\_grnd\_alignment\_text\_visible}, \texttt{ins\_ok\_text\_visible}).

\textbf{Run-004 random holdout.}
The pattern is consistent but the base model degrades more severely on this larger and more diverse holdout set.
The base model produces 4 invalid JSON/schema responses (yielding a validity rate of 0.96), 70 critical false positives (nearly all concentrated on \texttt{fcsmc\_intermediate\_result\_visible} with 60), and a sample exact match rate of only 0.03.

% Evidence: metrics_lora.json for Run-004 random → critical_false_positives_by_fact: fcsmc_final_go_result_visible=8

Across both holdouts, the LoRA adapter demonstrates three key properties.
First, it restores output format reliability: the base model occasionally produces malformed JSON or schema-invalid responses on the Run-004 holdout, while the LoRA adapter does not.
Second, it dramatically improves per-fact recall, as captured by seen $F_1$ rising from the range 0.50--0.57 (base) to 0.92--0.96 (LoRA).
Third, it reduces critical false positives by 64\% (Run-002) and 89\% (Run-004), though a residual set of false positives on completion-type facts (\texttt{fcsmc\_final\_go\_result\_visible}, \texttt{ins\_ok\_text\_visible}) persists.

\textbf{Comparison to 8-fact v1.}
% Evidence: experiment_inventory.md §4.1 vs §4.2
Moving from the 8-fact v1 to the 13-fact v2 ontology, the LoRA adapter's fact accuracy rises from 0.9150 to 0.9908 on Run-002-family holdouts, and the sample exact match rate rises from 0.36 to 0.88.
Critically, the v2 adapter reduces critical false positives (from 15 to 4 on Run-002-family) rather than increasing them, as the v1 adapter did relative to its base model.
This indicates that the combination of the expanded fact ontology, the Run-003 + Run-005$\times$2 data recipe, and the composition-rebalance strategy addressed the false-positive vulnerability that was present in the v1 line.

\subsection{Error Analysis}\label{sec:eval-errors}

% Evidence: benchmarks/qwen35_vlm_finetune/run003_plus_run005x2_vs_base_holdout_run002_newfacts_v1/fact_scores.csv
% Evidence: benchmarks/qwen35_vlm_finetune/run003_plus_run005x2_vs_base_holdout_run004_random_v1/fact_scores.csv
% Evidence: benchmarks/qwen35_vlm_finetune/run003_plus_run005x2_vs_base_holdout_run002_newfacts_v1/metrics_lora.json
% Evidence: benchmarks/qwen35_vlm_finetune/run003_plus_run005x2_vs_base_holdout_run004_random_v1/metrics_lora.json

To understand where the remaining errors occur and what patterns differentiate the base model from the LoRA adapter, this section provides a per-fact breakdown of the strongest configuration (Qwen LoRA on the 13-fact v2 ontology).

\subsubsection{Fact Categories by Difficulty}

The 13 visual facts can be grouped into three categories that reflect distinct levels of extraction difficulty.

\textbf{Group 1: Coarse panel presence (6 facts).}
Facts in this group indicate whether a particular cockpit panel or display page is currently visible: \texttt{tac\_page\_visible}, \texttt{supt\_page\_visible}, \texttt{fcs\_page\_visible}, \texttt{bit\_root\_page\_visible}, \texttt{fcsmc\_page\_visible}, and \texttt{hsi\_page\_visible}.
These are structural recognition tasks that rely on identifying large, visually distinctive panel layouts.

The Qwen LoRA adapter achieves perfect classification on all six facts on both the Run-002 newfacts and Run-004 random holdouts, with the single exception of \texttt{tac\_page\_visible} on Run-002 (two false negatives out of 50 samples; the TAC page is seen in 23 of 50 samples, so these represent missed detections rather than hallucinations).
% Evidence: Run-002 newfacts metrics_lora.json → tac_page_visible seen: tp=21, fn=2
The base model, by contrast, fails on several of these facts: it completely misses all five occurrences of \texttt{supt\_page\_visible} on Run-002 (seen $F_1 = 0.0$), hallucinates five false positives on \texttt{bit\_root\_page\_visible} on Run-002, and misses 18 of 94 occurrences of \texttt{fcs\_page\_visible} on Run-004 (seen recall $=0.81$).

\textbf{Group 2: Fine-grained state indicators (5 facts).}
Facts in this group capture subtle within-panel states: \texttt{fcs\_page\_x\_marks\_visible} (X-marks on the FCS page), \texttt{fcsmc\_intermediate\_result\_visible}, \texttt{fcsmc\_in\_test\_visible}, \texttt{fcsmc\_final\_go\_result\_visible} (three progressive states of the FCS-MC test sequence), and \texttt{hsi\_map\_layer\_visible} (whether the map overlay is active on the HSI).

The Qwen LoRA adapter achieves perfect classification on \texttt{fcs\_page\_x\_marks\_visible}, \texttt{fcsmc\_intermediate\_result\_visible}, and \texttt{hsi\_map\_layer\_visible} on both holdouts.
One false negative occurs on \texttt{fcsmc\_in\_test\_visible} on Run-004 (17 seen samples, 16 correctly identified).
The most persistent error source is \texttt{fcsmc\_final\_go\_result\_visible}, where the adapter produces 1 false positive on Run-002 and 8 false positives on Run-004 (against 60 seen occurrences).
% Evidence: Run-004 random metrics_lora.json → fcsmc_final_go_result_visible seen: tp=60, fp=8
This fact requires distinguishing a specific numeric readout within the FCS-MC panel, and the consistent false-positive pattern suggests that visually similar intermediate states are occasionally misclassified as the final GO result.

The base model's behaviour on this group is qualitatively different.
On Run-004, the base model generates 60 critical false positives on \texttt{fcsmc\_intermediate\_result\_visible} alone (against only 7 true seen occurrences), indicating a near-complete failure to distinguish this state.
The base model also shows poor recall on \texttt{fcs\_page\_x\_marks\_visible} (seen $F_1=0.0$ on both holdouts) and \texttt{fcsmc\_in\_test\_visible} (seen recall $\approx 0.33$--0.53).

\textbf{Group 3: Small-text recognition (2 facts).}
Facts \texttt{ins\_grnd\_alignment\_text\_visible} and \texttt{ins\_ok\_text\_visible} require the model to recognise small textual indicators on the INS panel.
These are the most fine-grained facts in the ontology and represent a near-OCR-level challenge for the 9B-parameter model.

The Qwen LoRA adapter performs strongly on \texttt{ins\_grnd\_alignment\_text\_visible}: perfect classification on Run-004 (69 seen, 31 not seen) and one false positive on Run-002 (42 seen, 7 not seen).
% Evidence: Run-002 newfacts metrics_lora.json → ins_grnd_alignment_text_visible: accuracy=0.98, seen_f1=0.9882
On \texttt{ins\_ok\_text\_visible}, the adapter produces 2 false positives on Run-002 (against 4 seen occurrences; these are samples where the OK text is not yet visible but the adapter asserts it is).
On Run-004, where \texttt{ins\_ok\_text\_visible} is never seen (0 of 100), the adapter correctly produces no false positives, achieving perfect accuracy.

The base model is effectively blind to both of these facts: seen $F_1 = 0.0$ on both holdouts for \texttt{ins\_grnd\_alignment\_text\_visible}, and seen $F_1 = 0.0$ for \texttt{ins\_ok\_text\_visible} (though the base model also never sees it on Run-004, so the $F_1$ metric reports 0.0 by construction).

\subsubsection{Error Taxonomy}

Aggregating across both holdouts for the Qwen LoRA adapter, the observed errors can be classified into three patterns:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Completion-fact false positives (majority).}
  The largest share of residual errors comes from \texttt{fcsmc\_final\_go\_result\_visible} and \texttt{ins\_ok\_text\_visible}, where the adapter occasionally asserts a completion/finalisation state that is not yet present.
  These are classified as \textit{critical} false positives in the benchmark because they carry direct downstream risk: if the tutoring system believes a step is complete when it is not, it may prematurely advance the procedure.

  \item \textbf{Isolated false negatives (minor).}
  Two false negatives on \texttt{tac\_page\_visible} (Run-002) and one on \texttt{fcsmc\_in\_test\_visible} (Run-004) represent missed detections of states that are actually present.
  These are less severe from a tutoring-safety perspective (a missed detection triggers a redundant prompt rather than skipping a step) but indicate residual recall gaps.

  \item \textbf{Small-text hallucination (minor).}
  One false positive on \texttt{ins\_grnd\_alignment\_text\_visible} (Run-002) represents the adapter falsely detecting the alignment status text.
  Given the small visual footprint of this indicator, this error is consistent with the expected difficulty profile.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Confusion Pattern Summary}

% Evidence: benchmark directories contain per-fact confusion matrix charts for both base and lora models
Per-fact confusion matrices (available as charts in each benchmark directory) reveal a systematic pattern in the base model's errors: the base model is prone to \textit{unidirectional hallucination} on facts that appear rarely in the training distribution.
For instance, \texttt{fcsmc\_intermediate\_result\_visible} appears in only 7 of 100 samples in the Run-004 holdout, yet the base model asserts it as \texttt{seen} in 60 additional samples where it is not present.
The LoRA adapter eliminates this class of error almost entirely, suggesting that the composition-rebalance and oversampling strategy effectively counteracted the base model's tendency to over-predict frequently co-occurring fact states.

\subsection{Runtime Infrastructure Readiness}\label{sec:eval-runtime}

% Evidence: replay_eval/fa18c_startup_v04/suite.yaml, cases/*/
% Evidence: logs/test_index/index.json
% Claim: C-RUNTIME-01

Beyond the VLM benchmark, the repository contains evidence of a functional replay-based evaluation infrastructure.
The replay evaluation suite at \texttt{replay\_eval/fa18c\_startup\_v04/} defines five test cases (\texttt{batteryon\_2min}, \texttt{Batteryon\_enginGenoff\_2min}, \texttt{fcs\_reset\_fcs\_bit\_2min}, \texttt{ins\_2min}, \texttt{noop\_2min}) with DCS-BIOS raw captures (\texttt{dcs\_bios\_raw.jsonl}) and per-frame sidecar files (\texttt{frames.jsonl}).
This infrastructure enables replay of recorded DCS sessions for offline testing of the telemetry pipeline, knowledge retrieval, and procedure execution logic without requiring a live DCS instance.

The runtime data collection pipeline records structured events through the \texttt{core/event\_store.py} module and indexes them under \texttt{logs/test\_index/index.json}.
At present, this pipeline captures telemetry snapshots, procedure step transitions, and VLM inference requests.
It does not yet implement the participant/session schema, quiz linkage, or NASA-TLX integration that would be required for a human-subject study; these are discussed as planned upgrades in Section~\ref{sec:eval-planned-upgrades}.

\section{Planned Runtime Data Collection and Human-Subject Evaluation}\label{sec:eval-planned}

The original research vision of this thesis, as articulated in the research proposal, includes a controlled human-subject evaluation of the grounded tutoring system in a DCS procedural learning context.
This evaluation has not yet been conducted.
The following subsections describe the planned evaluation design as it was formulated in the proposal, together with an assessment of the upgrades required to transition the current runtime infrastructure into a data-collection-ready state.
All statements in this section represent future work and are included for transparency about the intended trajectory of the research.

\subsection{Original Evaluation Vision}\label{sec:eval-planned-vision}

% Evidence: ThesisProposal1128.pdf; paper_context/proposal_summary.md §2–3
% Claims: C-PROPOSAL-02, C-HUMAN-02

The proposal envisions a between-subjects comparison of three conditions:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Video + notes baseline.} Learners study a pre-recorded video walkthrough and written checklist before and during the cold-start procedure, without any integrated tutoring system.
  \item \textbf{Ungrounded text-only LLM.} A language model provides step-by-step guidance via text, but without access to real-time simulator state, retrieved manual excerpts, or visual fact extraction.
  \item \textbf{Grounded RAG + LLM tutor.} The full system described in Chapters~\ref{ch:system-design}, \ref{ch:methodology}, and \ref{ch:implementation}: telemetry-grounded, retrieval-augmented, with structured visual fact extraction and overlay execution.
\end{enumerate}

The original target platform is the Meta Quest 3 with DCS-VR, providing in-headset guidance through step cards or overlay annotations.
The current system operates on the desktop/DCS (non-VR) configuration, so a VR deployment would need to be completed before this evaluation can proceed.
Target sample size is approximately $n = 5$--$10$ participants per condition, drawn from a beginner-to-intermediate population with limited prior F/A-18C cold-start experience.

\subsection{Planned Evaluation Metrics}\label{sec:eval-planned-metrics}

% Evidence: ThesisProposal1128.pdf; paper_context/proposal_summary.md §3.2
% Claims: C-HUMAN-01, C-HUMAN-03

The proposal specifies a multi-dimensional evaluation framework with the following planned metrics:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Immediate performance:} Procedure completion rate, procedural error rate, total task time, and dead-end count (number of times the participant becomes stuck and requires external intervention).

  \item \textbf{Workload:} NASA Task Load Index (NASA-TLX) administered immediately after the training session.
  % Evidence: Doc/Evaluation/fa18c_nasatlx_vr.md exists as a planned instrument

  \item \textbf{Learning:} Pre-test and post-test quiz scores measuring declarative knowledge of the cold-start procedure.

  \item \textbf{Retention:} A delayed post-test administered 2--4 weeks after the training session to assess knowledge retention.

  \item \textbf{Transfer:} Performance on a variant procedure (e.g., a modified cold-start sequence) to assess whether skills generalise beyond the trained scenario.
\end{itemize}

All of these metrics remain in the planning stage; no participant-level data has been collected.

\subsection{Required Runtime Data Collection Upgrades}\label{sec:eval-planned-upgrades}

% Claims: C-DATA-COLLECT-01

To support the planned human-subject evaluation, the current runtime data collection infrastructure requires several specific upgrades:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Participant and session schema.} The event store must be extended with a structured schema for participant demographics (anonymised ID, prior experience level, condition assignment), session metadata (date, duration, DCS version), and trial-level annotations.

  \item \textbf{NASA-TLX integration.} A post-session instrument for administering and recording NASA-TLX responses must be integrated into the runtime, either as an in-application form or as a linked external survey with session-level traceability.

  \item \textbf{Quiz linkage.} Pre-test and post-test quiz responses must be linked to participant and session identifiers to enable within-subject learning gain analysis.

  \item \textbf{Anonymisation pipeline.} All participant-identifiable data must pass through an anonymisation step before storage, in compliance with standard research ethics requirements for human-subject data.

  \item \textbf{VR deployment.} The runtime must be ported from the current desktop/DCS configuration to the Quest~3 / DCS-VR platform, including the in-headset overlay rendering and interaction handling described in the proposal.
\end{enumerate}

None of these upgrades have been implemented at the time of writing.

\subsection{Planned Experiment Design}\label{sec:eval-planned-design}

% Evidence: ThesisProposal1128.pdf; paper_context/proposal_summary.md §3

The planned experiment follows a standard pre-test / training / post-test / retention design:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Recruitment and screening.} Participants are recruited from a population with limited F/A-18C experience. A screening questionnaire establishes baseline DCS familiarity and assigns participants to conditions using stratified randomisation.

  \item \textbf{Pre-test.} A written quiz assesses declarative knowledge of the cold-start procedure before any training exposure.

  \item \textbf{Familiarisation.} A brief DCS cockpit familiarisation phase ensures all participants can locate basic controls and panels, reducing variance from platform unfamiliarity.

  \item \textbf{Training trial.} Participants execute the F/A-18C cold-start procedure under their assigned condition. The system records all telemetry, procedure state transitions, help requests, VLM inference calls, and (for the grounded condition) overlay execution events.

  \item \textbf{Post-test and NASA-TLX.} Immediately following the training trial, participants complete the NASA-TLX instrument and a post-test quiz identical in structure to the pre-test.

  \item \textbf{Retention test.} After a 2--4 week interval, participants return for a delayed post-test and, where feasible, a second cold-start trial to assess procedural retention.

  \item \textbf{Transfer task (optional).} A variant procedure is administered to assess the generality of learned skills.
\end{enumerate}

This design has been specified at the proposal level but has not been piloted or executed.

\subsection{Known Threats to Validity}\label{sec:eval-planned-threats}

Several threats to validity can be identified at the design stage, prior to any data collection:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Desktop vs.\ VR platform gap.}
  The current system operates on desktop DCS, not DCS-VR on Quest~3 as envisioned in the proposal.
  Results obtained in either platform may not generalise to the other without additional validation.

  \item \textbf{Benchmark performance does not imply learning outcomes.}
  The VLM benchmark results reported in Section~\ref{sec:eval-tech} demonstrate that the LoRA adapter extracts visual facts accurately.
  However, accurate visual fact extraction is a necessary but not sufficient condition for improving human learning outcomes.
  A human-subject study is required to establish whether the fact extraction performance translates into measurable procedural learning gains.

  \item \textbf{Small sample size.}
  The planned sample size of $n = 5$--$10$ per condition is small by the standards of controlled human-subject research and limits statistical power for detecting between-condition differences, particularly for noisy metrics such as completion time and error rate.

  \item \textbf{Beginner population generalisability.}
  The planned participant pool consists of individuals with limited DCS experience.
  Results may not generalise to experienced pilots or to other simulation platforms.

  \item \textbf{Single procedure.}
  The evaluation is designed around a single procedure (F/A-18C cold start).
  Whether the tutoring approach transfers to other procedural tasks within DCS or to other domains remains an open question.

  \item \textbf{Experimenter effects.}
  In a small-$n$, single-site study, experimenter behaviour during familiarisation and intervention can introduce systematic bias that is difficult to separate from condition effects.
\end{enumerate}

These threats do not invalidate the planned evaluation design, but they do bound the scope of conclusions that can be drawn from its eventual results.
Addressing them through careful protocol design, pre-registration of analysis plans, and explicit acknowledgement of limitations will be essential when the study proceeds.
